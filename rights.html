Sources & rights

An open library,
made carefully.

We are preparing a public-domain library that everyone will be able to read without an account. Preparing it means caring for the works, their editions and the people who made them.

The example you can explore today

The interactive demonstration uses newly written example text, an illustrative reading and our own typographic covers. It lets you follow a thought through CARE’s panes and back to its source. The examples are labelled as demonstration material.

Public reading, without a login

The planned public collection will contain carefully selected public-domain editions, with their status checked for the relevant territories. It is still being prepared. The public library page explains what is available now.

The development library contains a wider range of material. Its presence there does not mean it is available for public reading or redistribution.

The edition matters

An old work may have a recent translation, introduction or cover with separate rights. Our publication checks will therefore include the exact text and edition, as well as any images, quotations and accompanying material. Read the UK Intellectual Property Office’s guidance on copyright duration.

Acknowledgement matters, but it does not itself grant permission. Any reliance on quotation exceptions needs an assessment of the particular use. The IPO explains quotation and fair dealing and licences and permissions.

This page explains our publication approach and offers general UK guidance. It does not clear any particular work for reuse.
