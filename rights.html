Sources & rights

A reading should
know its sources.

Two original works. Two saved interpretations. One reading between them.

The source texts

Immanuel Kant, The Critique of Pure Reason. J. M. D. Meiklejohn’s English translation, identified against Project Gutenberg eText #4280. The retained reading has 20 source sections. It preserves CARE’s section divisions, rather than claiming to reproduce a particular print edition’s pagination.

David Hume, A Treatise of Human Nature. Original English, from Project Gutenberg eText #4705. The retained source identifies the 10 November 2012 transcription and has 27 reading sections. Gutenberg’s current transcription includes corrections, so some spelling, punctuation and footnote markers differ. We preserve the wording used by the saved CARE reading.

These underlying texts are assessed as out of copyright in the UK under the ordinary literary term: Hume died in 1776; Meiklejohn in 1902. Gutenberg also marks both texts public domain in the USA. This does not establish their status in every country. See UK copyright duration guidance.

The covers here are CARE’s own typographic designs. No modern publisher’s cover, introduction or translation has been included. Gutenberg is credited as the transcription source and does not endorse CARE. Gutenberg’s edition and attribution guidance.

The readings are interpretations

CH, CL, MC and WB contain saved AI-assisted CARE analyses, not words attributed to Kant or Hume. The showcase uses actual excerpts from these records, with one selected branch of their recorded references.

The public Kant reading is the preserved version WB_KPR@R001; Hume is WB_THN@R001. The saved diagnostic lateral, XR_KPR_THN · R01, was created on 16 August 2026 using those versions. A link records a relationship that the analysis declared; it does not prove the claim.

Both readings include AI passage alignments, which remain unreviewed. Kant’s supplemental grounding links 2,669 of its 2,692 chapter claims to passages; 23 remain explicitly unresolved. Its original source text and CARE interpretation are unchanged. Hash checks establish which saved records a link belongs to; they do not establish that the interpretation is correct. The reader does not invent missing connections.

The source texts’ public-domain status is distinct from the rights in CARE’s analysis, presentation and software. This public reading release does not apply the documentation repository’s licence to the whole private library or grant trademark rights. See the project’s current licensing boundaries.

A deliberately small public room

Anyone can read these two works and explore their saved lateral without signing in. This website contains a fixed export, not a connection to the development library. It has no book uploads or paid AI run controls. The separate beta entrance remains unconnected.

The export audit identifies the included versions and counts. Further works need their own edition and asset checks before joining the public collection.
